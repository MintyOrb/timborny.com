When someone leaves a critical meeting—a medical consultation, an estate planning session, or an emotionally charged therapy appointment—what they retain is radically vulnerable to decay.

The current technological response is naive: *"Record everything and produce a 10-page transcript."*

### The Flaw of Raw Transcripts

A transcript is not memory. In fact, a 45-minute verbatim transcript is practically unusable:
1. **High cognitive friction:** Nobody wants to re-read 8,000 words of conversational filler, false starts, and interruptions to find the dosage change or action item.
2. **Loss of salience:** In a transcript, an offhand comment about the weather looks visually identical to the oncologist's primary treatment recommendation.
3. **Severe privacy vulnerabilities:** Retaining raw recordings in cloud silos creates permanent liabilities for vulnerable moments.

### Turning Spoken Dialogue into Cognitive Artifacts

Memory requires synthesis, hierarchy, and spatial anchoring.

Our work on conversation memory apps focuses on:
- **Instant Distillation:** Transforming spoken dialogue immediately into concise decision cards, structured action plans, and key questions.
- **Local-First & Ephemeral Audio:** Processing securely on-device with zero server-side audio retention. The audio evaporates; only the structured cognitive notes remain.
- **Human Sovereignty:** You own the artifact completely.
