If you've ever walked out of an important doctor's visit, a planning meeting, or an emotional conversation, you know how quickly the details start slipping away. You remember the general tone, but the specific names, numbers, and next steps get fuzzy within hours.

The typical tech solution right now is: *record everything and hand the person a 10-page transcript.*

### Why Transcripts Don't Actually Help

Transcripts are overwhelming and noisy:
1. **Nobody wants to re-read them:** Digging through thousands of words of small talk, false starts, and tangents to find one specific instruction is painful.
2. **Everything looks equally important:** In a wall of text, an offhand comment about parking looks just as prominent as a critical medical instruction.
3. **Privacy risks:** Keeping intimate audio recordings in third-party cloud databases is a risk most people would rather avoid.

### What Actually Helps: Fast, Private Takeaways

What people actually need isn't a recording: they need clear, structured notes:
- What decisions were actually made?
- What are the concrete next steps?
- What questions are still unanswered?

And just as importantly, this should be done **local-first**: process the audio privately on your own device, extract the useful takeaways, and discard the raw audio.
